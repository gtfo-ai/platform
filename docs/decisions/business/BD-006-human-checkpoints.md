# BD-006 — Human checkpoints: questions always, plan approval by size policy

- **Status:** accepted (2026-08-28, Q3, Q8, Q10)
- **Date:** 2026-08-28
- **Relates to:** product/04, product/03 UJ-2

## Context
Full autonomy is the goal, but the brief explicitly wants refinement to raise questions and wait. Community experience (research/01) says the most valuable human checkpoint is *before* code is written, and that the cheapest one is answering a precise question.

## Decision
- Any agent stage may raise **questions**; the task pauses until answered via ticket comment, Slack thread or UI. Unanswered questions escalate to `Needs human` after **1 working day** by default (configurable per project), with one reminder before escalation — halfway through the working time to the deadline (WP-84).
- Only **mapped users** (platform account matched by email) can act: role `member` answers questions, role `maintainer` approves plans and knowledge proposals. Comments by unmapped users, bots or contractors are recorded but never trigger transitions (BD-022).
- **Plan approval** is a project policy: `never | above size (default L) | always`. Default: required for L/XL plans.
- **Amendment (WP-34, session 5 — as built, Q86):** a **shadow** task never asks for plan approval, whatever the policy, probation or risk class says. A shadow batch runs unattended by definition — nothing it produces reaches a provider (BD-021), its cost is bounded by the shadow budget and the org, project and task caps, and product/18's Observe level never asks for a plan — so an approval would park every ticket of the batch on a human for a decision that decides nothing. The skip is unconditional and stated at `packages/application/src/pipeline/saga.ts`; the alternative, one approval per batch, is recorded under Q86 if this is ever reversed.
- **Amendment (WP-56, session 8 — Q95 answered per its recommendation, not waited on):** an **approval** (plan or budget) expires on the same working-day calendar as a question and at the same default — **1 working day**, read from the template's limits, never from a new dial cell — and an expired approval escalates the task to `Needs human` exactly as an unanswered question does. The founder may reverse this; if so, the approval-expiry machinery is deleted rather than left unreachable (Q95). *Superseded for approvals by the 2026-11-07 amendment below; questions are unchanged.*
- **Amendment (2026-10-06, PROGRESS backlog 495 — as built; the decision was silent on what a rejection does):** a **rejected plan** sends the task back to Architecture as a **new attempt**, with the maintainer's reason as that attempt's return feedback, and spends one round of the `architecture_revisions` loop (BD-008, default 2); a rejection with the loop spent escalates the task to `Needs human`, the reason kept on the rejected attempt for whoever hands it back. A **rejected budget** is not a rejected plan: the task stops at `Needs human` and does not go round again, because a second plan would carry the same estimate (WP-28).
- **Amendment (2026-11-06, the owner on a live task — *"I'm blind and blind approve is no-go"*; data shape in TD-035):** an approval is a decision **about evidence**, so it carries three things and the screen shows all three beside the buttons:
  1. **Its subject** — the exact thing being approved, fixed when it was asked: for a plan, *the* plan version that was current at the request (not "the latest plan"); for a budget, the estimate and the threshold it crossed. A click can only approve the subject the person was shown; if a newer plan exists, the old approval cannot be approved — the newer plan is asked for on its own.
  2. **Why it was asked** — every reason that required it, not just the first: probation (task *n* of *N*), a risk class the plan touches (named), the plan's size at or above the threshold, a policy of `always`, an estimate above the budget threshold.
  3. **How to stop being asked for this reason** — e.g. probation ends after *N* completed tasks or when a maintainer ends it; a size threshold or the autonomy dial can be raised; a risk class's policy can be changed (product/19 §14 recommends keeping it). The platform states the lever; it never pulls it.

  The intent behind this: plan approval is **not** the goal of the product — refinement and plans should become good enough not to need it. It exists for exactly the cases where trust is not yet earned (a new project, a risky area, a large change), and each ask names which of those it is, so approvals become rarer as trust grows by a maintainer's visible choice rather than silently. An approval asked before this amendment whose subject or reasons were not recorded says **"not recorded"** — it is never back-filled with a guess — and a plan approval with no identifiable plan cannot be approved, only rejected (which asks for a fresh plan). Who may decide is unchanged (maintainer).
- **Amendment (2026-11-07, the product owner after AUT-6639 on Autix — its plan approval expired after 24 h and the task stopped; supersedes the WP-56 (Q95) amendment for approvals):** **an approval waits until a person decides it.**
  1. **Scope: every approval, plan and budget; questions keep their timeout.** An expired approval decided nothing: `Needs human` asks the same maintainer to act, and the usual hand-back is a new Architecture attempt, a paid run for an unchanged subject. Keeping the expiry for budgets alone would keep the whole machinery alive for one kind, which the Q95 amendment said must be deleted rather than left half-reachable. A question is different: the agent cannot go on without the information, any member may answer it, and `Needs human` is a real next step (answer, take over, return). That is this record's first bullet, unchanged.
  2. **While it waits.** The task reads **Waiting for approval** and is a **Needs you** item on the board, Home, the Inbox and the task header for as long as it waits. The Inbox shows *requested 3 h ago · waits for a maintainer*, not a due time. **Reminders** go through the existing notify duty (class `reminder`, quiet hours and the digest as for every reminder). The *n*-th comes at *(n − ½)* question timeouts after the request, on the working calendar, so the first is at today's instant. There are at most **five**: with the default, half a working day after the request and then once per working day, about a working week in all. After the fifth, chat is quiet, and the item stays in Needs you. A reminder for an approval decided meanwhile is withheld by the duty's existing re-check. There is no new key: `question_timeout` sets the pace (the Q95 amendment refused a new dial cell, and that still holds).
  3. **Approvals already pending: forward-only, no data rewrite.** From the build that ships this, no approval expires, including one requested under the old rule. Its stored `deadline_at` stays as written, as history, and is no longer read as an expiry. A timer an earlier build armed finds nothing to do. The API publishes every approval's `deadline_at` as `null`. An approval already `expired` stays expired, and its task stays where the expiry sent it (AUT-6639 included). A person's Retry or hand-back works as before.
  4. **The machinery is deleted**, as Q95 asked. Approval creation writes no deadline. The approval expiry command goes, and so does the approval half of the deadline recovery (both the overdue sweep and the backfill that gives an old row its first deadline). The `approval_timeout` job kind stays **parseable as a no-op**, so a queued timer ends `settled` and does not spend its retries. `expired` stays in the approval status and in `task.approval.decided`'s vocabulary, for history and replay.
  5. **Nothing is left without an ending.** `deadline.sweep` keeps its `recovery_row` exhaustion shape (TD-004's M7 amendment, WP-124/WP-156). A lost approval reminder is found by the reminder recovery row, which now looks for an approval whose **next** reminder is a grace overdue. An approval with its reminders spent has no timer at all, and its ending is a decision or the task's end. A waiting approval holds no run, offer, lease or budget reservation. It counts toward `max_tasks_in_pipeline` exactly as the `needs_human` it used to become did (`packages/infrastructure/src/pipeline/postgres-pipeline-store.ts:1268`), and the stranded-stage row never reads a `waiting_approval` task. So no limit changes.

  *Rejected:* a longer default (it still stops a task by itself); a per-project `approval_timeout` (the owner wants none); escalating while still pending (two *Needs you* states for one wait); daily reminders without a bound (noise nobody asked for). *Residuals:* a forgotten approval waits indefinitely and holds its pipeline slot, and what shows it is the Needs-you count and five reminders. A rollback to an earlier build gives these deadline-less approvals a deadline counted from then, through that build's backfill. Slice S-27.1.
- **Amendment (2026-11-10, the product owner's feedback round, M28; design in TD-037; slice S-28.8):** **a plan may be approved by the platform when its risk is low — opt-in, and only ever *less* asking than the project allows.** A project may turn on `policies.plan_approval_risk` (off by default). The plan then carries the Architect's **self-assessment** (confidence, risk level, risk factors from a closed list, reasons, open questions), and the platform computes its own **facts** about the plan, which the model cannot change. A plan whose size-policy ask is met by a low-risk, confident assessment within the project's limits is **approved by the platform**, and the approval row is still written — marked as decided by the platform, with its reasons — so the audit and the screen say who decided and why (BD-003). A missing assessment, any open question, a fact over a limit or a factor in `always_ask` keeps the wait. **Probation, risk classes and shadow are untouched**: each still asks (or, for shadow, never asks) exactly as above. *Residual:* the files a plan says it will change are the model's declaration; the facts bound it but cannot prove it.
- **Probation mode:** a new project requires plan approval for its first N tasks (default 5) regardless of size; a maintainer ends probation explicitly. Readiness level 0 keeps probation on (BD-026).
- Human **merge** is always required (BD-007).
- All other stages are autonomous by default. A project can add human stages anywhere.

## Rationale
Balances autonomy with the two places where human judgement has the highest leverage per minute: clarifying intent and blessing a large plan.

## Alternatives considered
- Always approve plans — slows small tasks; humans stop reading.
- Never ask, always assume — the failure mode every autonomous-agent report warns about.

## Consequences
- Questions inbox in UI; question/answer channels must be equivalent and deduplicated.
- Question response time becomes a tracked metric (product/16).
